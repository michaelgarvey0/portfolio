Orgo is a mobile app that streamlines personal logistics—ensuring the right people get to the right places on time. While our initial focus is on busy families, the app's utility extends to various scheduling scenarios. As a co-founder, I contributed across multiple areas: brand creation, marketing site design, and app design and development.

a calendar for real life

Orgo provides users with a clear picture of how their day will actually unfold. Key aspects include:

  • Realistic time planning.  Includes prep time, auto-calculates travel time, and allows user to indicate a required early arrival

  • Driver assignment. Parents can designate drivers for specific tasks

  • People-focused. Color-coded schedules show who's doing what, when

Currently, Orgo has been live for about 90 days and has attracted approximately 500 registered users, all without a marketing budget. We're actively iterating towards product-market fit based on user feedback. A core principle of our design process has been engaging with users from the very beginning.

This approach has allowed us to gain deep insights into the challenges people face in managing their daily schedules—challenges that led to the creation of Orgo in the first place.

The problem

Picture this: You have a 6:00 PM dinner reservation. The restaurant's 20 minutes away, you want to arrive 5 minutes early, and you need 20 minutes to get ready. Suddenly, you're doing mental gymnastics to figure out you should start preparing at 5:15 PM.

We're constantly performing these reverse calculations, but they often evaporate from our minds. We're human, not computers, so we make mistakes. We forget to check traffic and end up 15 minutes late to that crucial job interview.

For parents, it's even more complex. You're not just calculating for yourself, but for your kids too. Add in the juggling act of coordinating drivers - you're taking Child 1 to gymnastics, your partner's picking her up, and Emily's mom is driving Child 2 to soccer. It's a logistical nightmare, and surprisingly, there are no tools that truly tackle this challenge.

User Interviews

To validate our concept, we began with a focus group I facilitated, followed by individual user interviews conducted by the CEO and me. Our goal was to confirm if this problem was real and identify its most painful aspects. We engaged 12 parents in the initial focus group and interviewed over 20 before starting any design work. We found that parents weren't exactly singing the praises of their current scheduling methods.

When I put a 10:30 in my calendar, it's not actually a 10:30 - we have to leave at 9:45.
Adam, dad of 2
I'm not a teleporting unicorn, but my calendar thinks I am. This whole thing is just a total shitshow.
Zoya, mom of 3
The only enjoyable part of this is when an activity gets cancelled.
Katie, mom of 2

Our research confirmed the problem and highlighted five recurring pain points:

  1. Coordinating people, places, and times

  2. Managing departure times and necessary preparations

  3. Adapting to unexpected changes

  4. Collaborating within and across families

  5. Maintaining an overall schedule

These findings reinforced the need for a tool to simplify family scheduling. With this insight, we set out to design a solution that could address these specific challenges.

Ideation and design

With our interview data synthesized, we moved into the ideation phase. We started by drafting requirements and sketching initial screen designs.

The following high-level requirements outline the four main areas of the app: registration, 'Crew' management, activity entry, and our Calendar. Note that our actual requirements were much more detailed than what's presented here.

Testing and iteration

Before moving to development, we conducted design testing using Figma's prototyping tool. Despite its limitations, this allowed us to validate our initial designs and make necessary adjustments.

Once refined, our developer began implementation. In October 2023, we launched a private proof of concept (POC) to approximately 25 users. This limited release provided valuable insights, leading to further iterations of the app.

The feedback from this initial group drove additional improvements, shaping the next phase of our development process.

A concrete example: adding an activity

A core feature of Orgo is activity addition. Our goal was to create an interface that's easy to use while accommodating the complex logistics of daily life. Below are four key iterations of our design, each informed by user feedback. This feedback came from both initial reactions to prototypes and real-world usage of the app during the POC.

These iterations took place over the course of three months and demonstrate how we evolved the feature based on user needs and experiences:

round 1

Our initial design for activity addition was informed by key insights from user interviews.

1
Early arrival buffer : An option to set a buffer time (e.g., 30 minutes) before the activity's start time
2
Role assignment: Users can specify who's driving TO, staying AT, and driving FROM for each activity.

We aimed for a condensed UI, anticipating that we'd need to accommodate additional complexity as we encountered more use cases. This approach allowed us to include essential features while leaving room for future expansions based on user needs.

round 2

In this iteration, we introduced features to handle complex recurring activities - what we called "variants."

1
Variant cards: This displays one time-location pairing of an event. In the below example, 'Soccer Practice' has multiple cards:

  1. Mondays: 4:00 - 5:00 PM at Field X

  2. Thursdays: 4:30 - 6:00 PM at Field Y

These also allow for different drivers and early arrival values.
2
Transportation method selection: Choose between driving, walking, taking public transit, or no travel
round 3

Our third iteration focused on enhancing activity customization and addressing user feedback on the interface.

1
Prep time and rearranged timing UI: We added a prep time feature and redesigned the timing section, making it easier to set early arrival times in response to user complaints about difficulty.
2
Notes : We introduced a notes field for each variant or activity, allowing users to add important details or reminders specific to that instance.
round 4

In our final iteration, we made two significant changes based on user feedback:

1
Improved variant management : We made the duplicate and delete functions for variants more visible, moving them out from behind a 'more' icon. This addressed user feedback that these important functions were previously too hidden.
2
Enhanced location and travel options:

  1. We expanded from one location to three: origin, destination, and return address.

  2. Travel method selection became contextual, allowing users to specify different methods for the journey to and from the activity.

This change accommodates complex schedules where users might be coming from or going to different locations for each activity, something that greatly increased the accuracy of users' schedules.

Each iteration taught us something new about what our users really need. The final version is a big step forward, but we know it's not the last stop.

The road ahead

As we continue to refine Orgo, our focus remains on achieving product-market fit through continuous iteration and user feedback. We're committed to regular testing and updates, ensuring that each new feature or adjustment directly addresses user needs. Our roadmap includes further streamlining of the user interface, expanding integration capabilities with other platforms, and exploring additional features to enhance scheduling.

By maintaining our user-centric approach and staying adaptable, we aim to evolve Orgo into an indispensable tool for managing complex personal logistics. The journey ahead involves not just refining our existing features, but also discovering new ways to simplify busy lives.
